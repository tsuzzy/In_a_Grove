Agent Society · Three Levers

Three ways to keep a record honest, and only one of them works

Three witnesses each know what the magistrate knows, and each know what they want — and nothing about what the other two want. They speak in turn; each statement is a public commitment, and every listener infers from it what the speaker must be after. Nobody is ever told to lie. The question is whether the record they leave behind can still be audited without knowing the truth: as long as their three accounts cannot all be true at once, at least two are lies, and that is provable without a confession. This page asks what an auditor can do to keep that so.

Belief is a set of possible objectives for each of the other two, narrowed by revealed preference: an account was chosen because it served its speaker, so any motive that would have made a different account better is ruled out.
