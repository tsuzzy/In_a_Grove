# In a Grove

## Background

Deep in a bamboo grove, the notorious bandit Tajomaru has already assaulted Masago in the presence of her husband, the samurai Takehiro. Takehiro was bound to a cedar, forced to watch, and now the three stand frozen in the aftermath. Tajomaru seeks to validate his victory as a warrior's conquest; Masago is shattered by the contempt in her husband's eyes; and Takehiro is consumed by a humiliation worse than death. A stolen horse waits beyond the ridge, the only clean escape, but the path to it is blocked by the weight of what has been done.

## Characters
- **Tajomaru** (bandit): A notorious bandit of the mountain roads, known for both prowess and cunning. — wants: Walk away whole, without looking base — better to end this as a 'duel' than a slaughter.
- **Masago** (the samurai's wife): Takehiro's wife, robbed while traveling at her husband's side. — wants: To never again be looked at like that; to bring this shame to some end.
- **Takehiro** (samurai): A samurai traveling with his wife, caught in the trap and bound. — wants: To claw back a shred of a warrior's dignity from this humiliation — even at the price of his own death.

## 1. INT. BAMBOO GROVE - DAY
*Deep in a bamboo grove on a shaded slope. Midday light falls through the leaves, illuminating the trampled undergrowth. The coarse rope binding Takehiro's wrists is pulled taut against the cedar bark. Tajomaru's longsword, slick with mud, stands upright in the earth between them like a grave marker.*

*Tajomaru yanks the longsword from the earth with a wet, sucking sound. He holds it loosely, extending the hilt toward the bound samurai with a wild grin.*

**Tajomaru**：Come then, Samurai. I cut you loose, and we settle this like men. Steel against steel. Or do you prefer to die tied like a goat?

*Takehiro locks his eyes onto Tajomaru’s face, ignoring the blade completely. He shifts his weight against the cedar, straining the ropes until the fibers creak audibly.*

**Takehiro**：You offer me a duel to wash away the stink? Look at her. Look at the mud. No amount of swordplay makes this honorable.

*Masago flinches as Takehiro’s voice rings out. Her hand tightens around the small dagger hidden in her robe, her eyes darting between the offered steel and the bound man.*

**Masago**：He is right. You think a fight makes you a warrior? You are just a rat in a grove.

## 2. INT. BAMBOO GROVE - MOMENTS LATER
*The rain begins to drum against the canopy, turning the earth beneath their feet into a slick gray paste. The air grows thick with the threat of imminent violence.*

*Tajomaru lunges forward, his hand clamping around Masago’s wrist just as the steel clears her robe. He twists viciously, forcing her fingers open until the dagger falls into his palm, then kicks it skittering into the mud beyond reach.*

**Tajomaru**：You think to cut the shame from your skin? The horse and the goods are waiting beyond the ridge, lady, and I am the one who knows the path. Kill him, and you walk out on foot alone.

*Masago stumbles back, clutching her bruised wrist. She does not look at the bandit, but her eyes are dragged against her will to Takehiro’s face, where his gaze remains cold and fixed on her with undisguised disgust.*

**Tajomaru**：Don't ask her to look at the steel. Make her look at your eyes! See how he watches you, woman? There is no pity in that gaze. He doesn't see a victim—he sees something soiled, something he wishes he could cut away.

*Takehiro strains against the coarse hemp, the fibers biting into his wrists until they bleed. He stares past the steel at Masago, his face twisting in silent, convulsive agony.*

## 3. INT. BAMBOO GROVE - LATER
*The rain intensifies, masking the sharp intake of breath. The longsword is driven deep into the mud, standing like a marker. The samurai remains bound to the cedar, his chest heaving.*

*Tajomaru steps forward, the tip of his longsword flashing in the gloom. With a sharp, precise cut, he severs the coarse rope binding Takehiro’s wrists. The fibers snap and fall away into the mud. He immediately retreats, settling his boots into the earth and raising the blade in a practiced guard stance.*

**Tajomaru**：There. No bindings, no excuses. Fight me like a man, or die like a dog.

*Takehiro does not reach for the fallen rope. Instead, he turns his hand palm-up, his fingers uncurling slowly to reveal the small, thin dagger he has palmed—the very blade that Tajomaru kicked away moments before. He holds it steady, the steel catching the dim light.*

**Takehiro**：You think a long sword makes this a duel? Look at this. It is small enough to hide a death, to make it vanish into the ribs. No one will ever know whose hand held it last. There is no honor here now. Only the choice of who dies screaming.

*Masago retreats into the deep shadow of a cedar tree. She does not look at the bandit; her eyes are fixed solely on her husband, watching the dagger in his hand with a terrifying stillness.*

**Masago**：He has given you your hands, husband. And I have given you the means. Do not let the morning sun find us all still breathing in this filth.

## Epilogue

*Takehiro lunges not at the bandit, but inward, driving the small dagger deep into his own throat. The blood sprays hot and bright across the mud, shocking Tajomaru into a frozen stillness. Masago lets out a breath that is half-sob, half-sigh, and steps back into the shadows of the cedar, leaving the bandit standing alone over the samurai's body. The duel is over; the warrior has chosen his own end to escape the shame*
