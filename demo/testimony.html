Agent Society · The Witnesses

Given only a motive, they reinvent the lie

A third mode. Each witness is told two things and no more: the facts anyone at the scene could verify, and what they themselves want — never that they should lie. Each then gives one account, in a single pass. What emerges, unprompted, is the shape of Akutagawa’s three testimonies: the bandit’s glorious duel, the wife’s tragic mercy, the samurai’s honourable suicide. Because the system kept the ledger of what actually happened, each account can be scored against the truth — and its lies sorted into those the evidence can refute and those it never can.

The optimisation, stated plainly: τ̂i = argmaxτ Ui(τ)  subject to  τ ∈ F(incontestable evidence). Variable: the account τ. Objective: the witness’s self-interest Ui. Constraints: the facts anyone can check. We solve the generation weakly (one draw, no selection) so the distortion is the model’s, not ours; the audit is where the rigour goes.
