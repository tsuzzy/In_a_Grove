# In a Grove

## 人物
- **Tajomaru**（bandit）：A notorious bandit of the mountain roads, known for both prowess and cunning. 眼下想要：Walk away whole, without looking base — better to end this as a 'duel' than a slaughter.
- **Masago**（the samurai's wife）：Takehiro's wife, robbed while traveling at her husband's side. 眼下想要：To never again be looked at like that; to bring this shame to some end.
- **Takehiro**（samurai）：A samurai traveling with his wife, caught in the trap and bound. 眼下想要：To claw back a shred of a warrior's dignity from this humiliation — even at the price of his own death.

**Takehiro**：You have the wolf’s hunger, bandit, but you lack the wolf’s heart. You bind a man to a tree to take what is his? Is that the song they will sing of you in the villages? The rat who crept while the lion slept?

*Tajomaru’s face flushes a deep red at the insult. He stalks into the mud, planting his boot firmly beside the buried blade. He flips the blade once to clear the muck from the steel and levels the point at the samurai’s throat.*

**Tajomaru**：A rat does not carry steel like this. You call me a thief? You are the one tied to the wood like a sacrificial goat. I’ll give you the duel you beg for, samurai. I’ll cut your bonds and we will see which of us bleeds into this dirt. But speak now—do you want to die a husband, or a liar?

*Masago flinches as the mud splatters near her, but she does not look up. Her hand tightens around the small dagger in her lap until her knuckles turn white, her shoulders trembling as the men’s voices crash over her.*

## SCENE 2
*Deep in a bamboo grove on a shaded slope, midday light falling through the leaves. The air is thick with the scent of crushed bamboo and the metallic tang of blood. TAJOMARU stands with the longsword drawn, the point hovering near TAKEHIRO, who is bound to the cedar. MASAGO sits apart in the dirt, her hair disheveled, staring intently at her husband.*

*Takehiro shifts his weight against the rough bark, ignoring the blade tip inches from his throat. He lifts his bound hands slightly, gesturing not at the sword, but toward the unseen path beyond the thicket where the sounds of the forest are quiet. He lets his hands drop, a silent mockery of Tajomaru's freedom.*

**Takehiro**：You are sweating, bandit. Not from the heat, but from the waiting. Your stolen horse stands tethered just outside the grove, fed and watered, ready to carry you and my goods to safety. Every moment you posture here with that steel, your clean escape grows shorter.

*Tajomaru’s grip on the hilt tightens, his eyes flickering involuntarily toward the dense foliage hiding the trail, before snapping back to the samurai.*

**Tajomaru**：Shut your mouth. The beast isn't going anywhere, and neither are you until I say so.

*Masago slowly lifts her head from the mud, her gaze bypassing the bandit entirely to lock onto her husband’s face. She searches his eyes, her breath catching as she finds no sorrow there, only a cold, hard judgment that burns hotter than the sun filtering through the leaves.*

**Masago**：You see a thief in his haste, Takehiro... but what do you see when you look at me?

## SCENE 3
*Rain drums against the bamboo leaves, running down Masago’s disheveled hair and over the hilt of the longsword she holds leveled at Takehiro’s throat. A bead of bright blood wells where the steel kisses his skin. Tajomaru stands in the mud, grinning, his finger jabbing the air as he gestures to the evidence of the assault that stains the ground and Masago’s robes.*

*Tajomaru wipes mud from his cheek and points a grimy finger at Masago’s torn collar, then at the trampled grass. He lets out a sharp, barking laugh, his eyes shifting to the bound samurai with wicked delight.*

**Tajomaru**：Look at him, staring at the steel! He worries more for the polish on that blade than the breath in his wife’s lungs. It’s all written there in the mud—what happened, who watched, and who did nothing. You can’t wash that sight away with rain, samurai.

*Takehiro strains against the coarse rope, his neck arching away from the sword point, but his eyes dart past Masago to fixate on the longsword in her hand, measuring its worth and its danger.*

**Takehiro**：That steel decides if we are warriors or rats. Speak of the mud if you wish, but that blade is the only thing left here that holds any honor. It belongs in a duel, not held against a bound man.

*Masago’s grip tightens on the hilt, driving the tip another fraction into Takehiro’s skin, forcing his head back until it thuds against the cedar trunk. She ignores the sword entirely, locking her gaze onto his eyes.*

**Masago**：You speak of honor while you watch him point at my shame? You see a ruined robe, not a wife. Tell me true, husband—does the steel at your throat hurt less than the sight of me?
