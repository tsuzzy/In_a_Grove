The Ring and the Book — The Pope's File

Rome, 1698: Count Franceschini's murder of his wife. Unlike the In a Grove page, there is no whodunit here. Guido confesses to the killing twice, in Books V and XI, and every feasible account agrees on what he did. What is disputed is what that act was: what happened at the inn, whose hand held the pen, and why she fled. Establish the Old Yellow Book documents on the left one at a time and watch the three witnesses re-optimize, each on their own. No one can hear anyone else: the three optimization problems are independent and interact only through the shared feasible set. Each takes one account from the same set, and the choices collide.

These three are a departure: their objective is the audience, not their own image. They are here because, if people with nothing at stake can reproduce the parties' positions, the disagreement is not caused by self-flattery.

Where they conflict "—" = the witness is indifferent, so makes no claim here

Why the "—" is necessary. Pompilia's utility only requires that the pen was not in her hand, so "in Guido's hand" and "in Caponsacchi's hand" are exactly equal for her. Printing the raw argmax would have her accuse the priest: an artifact of string ordering, and the exact opposite of Book VII. So a witness counts as making a claim only when all of their optimal accounts agree on it.
