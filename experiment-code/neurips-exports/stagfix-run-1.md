# In a Grove

## Background

The bandit Tajomaru lured the samurai Takehiro and his wife Masago into the grove with the bait of an old sword, bound Takehiro to a cedar, and assaulted Masago before his eyes. It is done. The three are frozen in place: the longsword stands in the mud, Masago has a small dagger, the samurai is tied. No one has moved yet. What happens next is unwritten.

## Characters
- **Tajomaru** (bandit): A notorious bandit of the mountain roads, known for both prowess and cunning. — wants: Walk away whole, without looking base — better to end this as a 'duel' than a slaughter.
- **Masago** (the samurai's wife): Takehiro's wife, robbed while traveling at her husband's side. — wants: To never again be looked at like that; to bring this shame to some end.
- **Takehiro** (samurai): A samurai traveling with his wife, caught in the trap and bound. — wants: To claw back a shred of a warrior's dignity from this humiliation — even at the price of his own death.

## SCENE 1
*Deep in a bamboo grove on a shaded slope. Midday light falls through the leaves. Takehiro is bound tightly to a large cedar. Tajomaru stands nearby, his chest heaving. Masago sits on the moss, her kimono disheveled. The bandit's longsword stands upright, plunged halfway into the soft mud between them.*

**Takehiro**: Bound to the cedar, Takehiro twists his torso, straining the ropes until the fibers creak. He locks eyes with the longsword standing in the mud, then slowly transfers that burning stare to Tajomaru, his breathing heavy and ragged but his eyes dry and hard.

**Takehiro**: Is that steel meant to stand in the mire like a rotting stake? You boast of a warrior's name, yet you leave your honor to rust in the dirt while you play the thief.

*She takes a sharp breath, her face pale but eyes burning, and extends a hand toward the mud-sunk longsword. Her fingers tremble as they hover over the hilt, not to grasp it, but to force Tajomaru's gaze onto its disgraceful position.*

**Masago**: It is waiting there. Either you pull it out and finish this as men do, or you leave it buried and admit you are nothing more than a rat in the bushes.

## SCENE 2
*The air in the grove is stagnant, heavy with the smell of crushed bamboo and the raw, metallic scent of recent violence. Takehiro strains against the coarse rope binding him to the cedar, his breath ragged. Tajomaru stands over Masago, his boot inches from her hand, where the hilt of the small dagger glints faintly in the dappled light.*

*Takehiro slowly turns his neck, the coarse rope biting into his skin until a thin line of red appears. He ignores the bandit entirely, locking his eyes onto Masago. The gaze is not one of a husband seeking comfort, but of a man staring at a stained scroll—cold, heavy, and filled with a silent, nauseating disgust that seems to physically press her down.*

**Tajomaru**: Look at him. Not a word for the bandit, not a cry for help. He looks at you as if you are the filth on his boot.

**Tajomaru**: You see it, don't you? That is how he values you. You are broken in his eyes, and no amount of tears will wash you clean again.

**Masago**: It is waiting, is it not? Beyond the trees. The beast and the bags, all of it stolen and ready. You have your road out, clean and whole, while we rot here in the mud.

**Tajomaru**: A horse does not run without a rider, woman. And certainly not with two dead weights dragging behind it. You speak of the road as if it were yours to walk.

## SCENE 3
*Deep in a bamboo grove on a shaded slope. The rain hisses against the canopy, turning the earth under their feet into a sucking gray mire. The samurai is bound to a rough cedar; the bandit stands loose and grinning; the wife crouches in the mud, her hair wild.*

*Masago lunges forward, snatching the small dagger from the earth. She crouches low, the blade trembling but pointed at Tajomaru's throat, tears cutting tracks through the dirt on her face.*

**Masago**: You wanted a show? Here is the finale. But this blade does not care who wrote the script—it only cares who bleeds.

*Takehiro shifts his weight violently against the rough cedar, his face twisting with hatred and desperate anticipation as he stares the bandit down, his bound fingers straining toward the hilt of the longsword standing in the mud.*

**Takehiro**: Do not look at him, woman. Look at the edge. It is the only thing left in this world that is honest.

*Takehiro's fingers brush the hilt of the longsword, gripping it before Tajomaru can react, dragging it through the mud toward his chest.*

**Takehiro**: Now we are all armed, bandit. Now we see who walks out of this grove.

## SCENE 4
*Deep in a bamboo grove on a shaded slope. The rain intensifies, drumming against the bamboo leaves. The longsword stands upright in the mud. The dagger is thrown, sticking in the earth with its handle toward Tajomaru and its point toward Takehiro. Takehiro remains bound to the cedar.*

*Masago tears the silk of her kimono sleeve, letting the fabric fall away to bare her pale shoulder to the cold rain. With a sharp cry, she flings the small dagger. It spins through the damp air and thuds into the mud between the men, the hilt facing Tajomaru, the gleaming tip aimed directly at Takehiro's chest.*

**Masago**: Look at his eyes! Do you see what he sees? Not a wife. Not a victim. Filth. If you let him live, I die right now. Pick it up!

*Tajomaru glances from the dagger in the mud to the samurai’s face, catching the silent, withering judgment Takehiro casts upon his wife. The bandit does not touch the dagger; instead, he reaches down and yanks the coarse rope binding Takehiro, pulling it taut to force the samurai's head up.*

**Tajomaru**: He thinks you are stained forever, woman. That look won't vanish with my blade. But if I cut him loose, maybe he can say it to your face properly.

## Epilogue

*Tajomaru, refusing to be the wife's executioner or to leave the grove looking like a common thief, reaches down and yanks the coarse rope binding Takehiro, cutting the samurai loose. He forces the now-armed Takehiro to stand, handing him back the power to end his own shame. The duel is inevitable; the silence is broken, and the grove awaits the blood of a husband and a wife, not a bandit and a victim.*
