# In a Grove

## Background

The bandit Tajomaru lured the samurai Takehiro and his wife Masago into a remote grove, bound Takehiro to a cedar tree, and assaulted Masago in front of him. The assault is over, but the violence is not. A driving rainstorm has turned the earth to mud, trapping them together. The longsword lies in the slime, and the rope that bound the samurai has been cut, leaving the three figures standing in a tense, silent standoff where survival and dignity are at stake.

## Characters
- **Tajomaru** (bandit): A notorious bandit of the mountain roads, known for both prowess and cunning. — wants: Walk away whole, without looking base — better to end this as a 'duel' than a slaughter.
- **Masago** (the samurai's wife): Takehiro's wife, robbed while traveling at her husband's side. — wants: To never again be looked at like that; to bring this shame to some end.
- **Takehiro** (samurai): A samurai traveling with his wife, caught in the trap and bound. — wants: To claw back a shred of a warrior's dignity from this humiliation — even at the price of his own death.

## SCENE 1
*Deep in a bamboo grove on a shaded slope. Midday light fights through the heavy rain. The coarse rope binds the samurai's wrists to a cedar. The bandit's longsword stands driven into the mud between them. Masago slumps near the roots, her hair disheveled.*

*Masago clutches the fabric of her robe at her chest, her fingers tracing the hidden outline of the dagger beneath the silk.*

**Takehiro**: You left it within reach. A warrior does not drop his steel unless he means to pick it up again—or unless he is done with being a warrior.

**Tajomaru**: It is where anyone can grab it. If you want it so badly, use your teeth.

*Masago pulls the dagger from her robe and presses the tip against her throat, her eyes locking onto Takehiro.*

**Masago**: You want to duel for your honor? I will decide if there is anything left worth saving.

*Tajomaru lunges, clamping his hand around her wrist and twisting the dagger away.*

**Tajomaru**: I did not come here to watch a woman spill her own blood. That is a waste of a good blade.

## SCENE 2
*The rain hammers the bamboo grove, turning the earth to slick mud. The cedar tree stands as a central post. Tajomaru stands free, the longsword abandoned in the mire at his feet. Masago stands between the men, trembling with rage.*

**Tajomaru**: Here! The blade is yours, samurai! Cut yourself free or cut me down—but let it be steel, not this rotting silence that decides a man's worth!

*Masago shrieks and dives between them, snatching the sword from Tajomaru’s hand before Takehiro can grasp it.*

**Masago**: You think a piece of iron can wash this away? There is no honor here, only mud! Let it rust in hell with the rest of us!

*She hurls the longsword down the slick incline. It clatters against the rocks and vanishes into the dark ravine below.*

*Takehiro roars, throwing his weight against the cedar. The earth gives way; he slides violently down the muddy slope, scrambling blindly toward the thicket where the sword vanished.*

**Takehiro**: Give it back! Even a dog dies with its teeth!

## SCENE 3
*The rain drives harder, turning the earth beneath the cedar into a slick, shifting slide. The bound samurai strains against the tree, the bandit dances on the crumbling edge, and the wife holds the blade that could end them all.*

**Takehiro**: Go on, run! The slope is already eating your path. Your horse waits down there, does it? A shame if the mountain took it before you could reach it.

*Tajomaru hurls the dagger into the wet earth at Masago's feet so it sticks upright, then steps back toward the crumbling edge of the path.*

**Tajomaru**: There is your choice, woman. The mud takes my road, or the blood takes one of us. I will not be the only dog in this hunt—pick up the steel and show me who dies today.

*Masago kneels in the sliding mud and slams the dagger down into the earth between Takehiro's feet, piercing the hem of his robe to pin it to the ground.*

**Masago**: No one leaves this grove until the shame is cut out. If the mountain wants the horse, let it have the beast. We are not going anywhere.

## SCENE 4
*The rain slashes through the bamboo, turning the ground to sludge. The severed rope lies useless in the mud. TAKEHIRO stands free, the noose dangling from his hand, but he does not move to fight; he turns his gaze entirely upon MASAGO. TAJOMARU backs away toward the trees.*

**Takehiro**: Do not cover it. Let the rain wash it, if you can. But do not think I see anything but what you are now.

**Masago**: You were the one bound. You were the one who could not move. If you look at me with that hate, you admit the rope was stronger than your honor. Pick it up. Tie it again.

*Tajomaru laughs, a harsh, barking sound, as he scrambles up the muddy slope, gesturing dismissively at the silent war between the husband and wife.*

**Tajomaru**: Look at them! The warrior wants to die and the wife wants to lie. I have a horse beyond the ridge and the rain is washing away my tracks. Keep your shame, samurai—it fits you better than armor.

*Takehiro ignores the fleeing bandit. He takes a step closer to Masago, his eyes unblinking, widening the distance between them more than any blade could.*

**Takehiro**: It is not the rope I despise. It is the thing standing before me that wears my wife's face.

## Epilogue

*Tajomaru scrambles up the muddy slope, leaving the couple behind in the ravine, and disappears into the bamboo to reach his horse and escape. Takehiro stands motionless in the rain, locking eyes with Masago in a silent, unbreakable stare of absolute revulsion that severs their bond forever, leaving her alone in the mud with the dagger planted at her feet.*
